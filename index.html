Vocabulary: Building Nouns

To talk about achievements and abilities professionally, we often turn verbs and adjectives into nouns using specific endings called suffixes.

The 5 Suffix Formulas 🧪

Verb + -ment

Turns an action into a thing.

develop ➡️ development

Adj + -ness

A state/quality. ('y' to 'i').

happy ➡️ happiness

Adj + -ity

Notice the stress shift!

able ➡️ ability

Verb + -tion/-sion

The process of doing an action.

educate ➡️ education

Verb + -er/-or

A person who does the action.

teach ➡️ teacher

Expressing Ability

In the audio, we heard phrases like "he *could* play" and "he *managed to* learn". Why didn't they just use "can" for everything? Let's decode the grammar of abilities.

The Timeline of Ability ⏳

🟢

General Present

Things you know how to do right now.

can + base verb
"I can speak English."
am/is/are able to + base verb
"She is able to solve the puzzle." (More formal)
⏮️

General Past

Things you knew how to do in the past.

could + base verb
"Mozart could play the violin."
was/were able to + base verb
"They were able to swim well."
🔥
🏆

Difficult Achievement

Doing something specific that was hard to achieve. (Do NOT use 'could' for a single successful past event!).

manage to + base verb
"I managed to finish the marathon."
succeed in + verb-ing
"He succeeded in learning Icelandic."

"What's something you managed to figure out that makes you feel really smart today?"

🔧 Repair It! (Confident Communicator)

If you don't understand your podcast guest, DON'T switch to Spanish! Use these to keep the conversation flowing:

  • "Sorry, what was that?"
  • "I'm sorry, I didn't quite catch that."
  • "What do you mean by [word]?"
